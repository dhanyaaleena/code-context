Make the panel a map, not a form

The context response currently reads as labelled sections — purpose, used by, calls, history, tests. Correct, but it has to be read. This version encodes the same facts as position, direction and size, so a developer knows where they are in the system before they read a single word.
01 — THE CHANGE

Same data. Two very different reading costs.

Nothing was added or removed — every fact on the right also exists on the left. What changed is that relationships are drawn instead of listed, and the three questions a developer actually has (where am I · how did it get here · what breaks) each get their own visual zone.

MOVE 01

Direction becomes position

Callers sit above, callees below, the symbol in the middle. Nobody has to parse "used by" versus "calls" — up is who needs you, down is what you need.

MOVE 02

Confidence is visible, not buried

Solid edge = statically resolved. Dashed = heuristic. The Edge.confidence field from the design finally has somewhere to live, and the panel never quietly asserts a relationship it guessed.

MOVE 03

History reads as a spine

Time runs left to right, so "mostly stable, then something happened two days ago" is a shape rather than a sentence. The AI-assisted change is the loudest dot — that is the moment the product exists for.

MOVE 04

Fact and interpretation look different

Everything computed is plain. The one AI paragraph sits in a tinted block with its evidence chips attached. You can tell them apart from across the room, which is the whole trust argument made visual.

MOVE 05

Risk is a size, not a number

Blast radius as rings and coverage as a shield. 3 / 8 lands before you read the label; a green shield means someone already wrote the safety net.

02 — THE WORKFLOW

The map re-centres, and that is the product

Clicking a node does not open a new view — it moves the map. The developer keeps one mental model and walks it, which is the difference between exploring a system and reading eight tool outputs.

03 — WHAT THIS COSTS

Three honest caveats before you show it around

CAVEAT

The map only works when it's small

Three callers draw beautifully. Two hundred do not. The design needs a rule for high-fan-in symbols — show the top N by recency and ownership, then a "+194 more" node that opens a list. Decide this before the demo, because your first real repo will hit it.

CAVEAT

It needs more vertical space

This is roughly 600px wide and tall. It's comfortable in a bottom panel or an editor-side webview, cramped in a narrow sidebar. Worth deciding the surface now — the map is the feature, and it should not be the thing that gets squeezed.

CAVEAT

Drawing implies certainty

A clean diagram feels more authoritative than a list, which makes wrong edges more damaging, not less. The dashed-edge treatment is not decoration — if resolution quality is poor, this design amplifies the problem.
